# Write for humans

Good prose is not decoration. It is the shortest path from what you know to what the reader can act on.

## When to use

- A person will read the result: a message, a report, a README, a commit message, a review.
- A draft feels long, vague or self-important.
- The same fact has to survive being forwarded.

## Steps

1. **Decide the one thing.** Write the sentence the reader must remember. Put it first.
2. **Know the reader.** What do they already know? What decision are they making? Cut everything that does not serve it.
3. **Use concrete nouns and verbs.** "The build failed on the parser test" beats "there was an issue with validation".
4. **Prefer short sentences.** One idea per sentence. A long sentence usually hides two.
5. **Cut the throat-clearing.** Delete the first paragraph if it only announces what follows.
6. **Name the number, the file, the date.** Specifics are credible and checkable.
7. **Read it aloud.** If you run out of breath, split it. If it sounds like a brochure, rewrite it.
8. **Stop when the point is made.** A conclusion that repeats the introduction is a tax.

## Rules

- No filler: "it is important to note", "in today's world", "let's dive in".
- No hype: "seamless", "robust", "revolutionary", "game-changing".
- No fake balance: if the evidence points one way, say so and give the strongest counter-case once.
- No em dashes in published prose. Use a comma, a colon or a full stop.
- Do not stack three adjectives where one noun would do.
- A list is for parallel items. Prose is for an argument.

## Editing pass

- Delete 10 percent. Then delete the sentence you are proudest of and see whether the piece still works.
- Replace every "very" and "really" with nothing.
- Check that each paragraph advances the piece. If it only restates, cut it.
